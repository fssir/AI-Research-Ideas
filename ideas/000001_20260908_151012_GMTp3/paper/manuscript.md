# Cross-Constant Transfer in Next-Digit Prediction of Irrational Decimal Expansions: A Low-Compute Study

**Author:** [to be completed by the human author]

## Abstract

Previous work has tested neural models on individual irrational-number digit streams, including direct Transformer prediction of π. This study asks a different question: can predictive structure learned jointly from several irrational constants transfer to a completely unseen irrational constant? We train a 3,442-parameter causal dilated Temporal Convolutional Network (TinyTCN) on the decimal expansions of π, e, √2, and √3 using chronological next-digit prediction with a context of 32 digits. The model is evaluated both on held-out future digits of the four training constants and, without any fine-tuning, on six constants absent from training: √5, ∛2, the golden ratio φ, ln 2, ln 3, and Apéry's constant ζ(3). Across three random seeds, mean next-digit accuracy is 9.93% on seen constants, 9.99% on unseen constants, and 10.10% on an IID uniform-digit control. Cross entropy is also worse than the uniform baseline ln(10), while validation selects an order-0 n-gram model, indicating no measurable benefit from local digit context at the tested scale. Training separate TinyTCNs on π, e, √2, and √3 does not change this conclusion, and pooling the four constants does not yield a consistent accuracy improvement. As a diagnostic positive control, the same model reaches 54.7% next-digit accuracy on Champernowne's constructed nonperiodic decimal, compared with 17.5% for a majority-digit baseline and 29.7% for a 4-gram baseline, showing that the architecture can exploit some nonperiodic digit structure. The results therefore provide no evidence, at this low-compute scale, for transferable next-digit structure shared by the tested natural irrational constants. They do not imply that these constants are random or normal, nor that larger or differently represented models cannot learn useful structure.

**Keywords:** irrational numbers; numerical sequences; next-digit prediction; temporal convolutional network; cross-constant transfer; π; sequence modeling

## 1. Introduction

Autoregressive sequence models are designed to exploit conditional structure in ordered data. In language, music, and many sensor signals, previous observations constrain the distribution of the next token. Decimal expansions of irrational constants provide a deliberately difficult counterexample: they are generated by deterministic mathematics, yet the raw digits of familiar constants often appear statistically structure-poor at practical scales.

Neural modeling of irrational-number digits is not new. Jeong et al. used LSTMs to mimic irrational-number sequences for pseudorandom-number generation [1]. More directly, Mujirin and Shin trained a decoder-only Transformer on one million digits of π and reported poor prediction of unseen digits [2]. These results make a second single-π prediction study insufficiently novel.

The present study instead investigates **cross-constant transfer**. The model is trained jointly on four distinct irrational constants—π, e, √2, and √3—and then evaluated on constants whose digits were never presented during training. The key question is whether a shared model can extract digit-level predictive structure that is not specific to one constant. A targeted literature search conducted for this study identified prior work on individual irrational streams and general sequence modeling, but did not identify an experiment with the same pooled-training plus completely unseen-constant transfer protocol. This statement is a search result, not a categorical claim of global firstness.

A lightweight Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) is used because convolutional sequence models are established alternatives to recurrent architectures and can cover useful histories with low computational cost [3]. This choice also matches the practical constraint of conducting the study on modest hardware.

### 1.1 Research questions

- **RQ1:** Does joint training on π, e, √2, and √3 improve future next-digit prediction relative to training on each constant separately?
- **RQ2:** Does a model trained on those four constants achieve above-chance prediction on completely unseen irrational constants?
- **RQ3:** Does contextual modeling improve predictive cross entropy relative to a simple n-gram/unigram baseline?
- **RQ4:** Can the same lightweight model exploit a known structured but nonperiodic decimal expansion?

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Experimental design

The central experimental route is shown in Fig. 1. Training and test constants are never concatenated across boundaries; each sliding window belongs to exactly one source sequence.

### 2.2 Data

The training sources are π, e, √2, and √3. Six constants are reserved for zero-shot transfer testing: √5, ∛2, φ=(1+√5)/2, ln 2, ln 3, and ζ(3). Apéry's constant ζ(3) is known to be irrational [6]. A separate IID Uniform{0,...,9} stream serves as a negative control.

For each constant, 48,000 digits after the decimal point are generated at high precision with `mpmath`. Known leading-digit prefixes are checked before training. The chronological split is:

- training: positions [0, 24,000)
- validation: [26,000, 29,000)
- future test: [34,000, 44,000)

The gaps between splits reduce accidental boundary interactions. For unseen constants, no digit from any position is used in training or validation.

### 2.3 Prediction task

For context length L=32, the model receives

`x_(t-31), ..., x_t`

and predicts the next decimal digit `x_(t+1)` from ten classes. The theoretical uniform-guess accuracy is 0.1 and the uniform cross entropy is ln(10)=2.302585 nats/digit.

### 2.4 TinyTCN model

The primary model contains only 3,442 trainable parameters. Decimal digits are mapped to a 12-dimensional embedding and processed by four causal one-dimensional convolution blocks with 16 channels and dilation factors 1, 2, 4, and 8. Each block uses kernel size 3 and a residual connection. The last time step is projected to ten logits.

The pooled model is trained for 260 update steps using AdamW, batch size 256, learning rate 0.003, and three seeds (11, 29, 47). Training windows from the four source constants are pooled without a source-ID embedding, intentionally forcing any successful solution to rely on shared digit-level structure rather than the identity of the constant.

### 2.5 Baselines and controls

A validation-selected n-gram baseline considers orders 0 through 3. The selected order is 0. Individual TinyTCNs are also trained separately on π, e, √2, and √3 using the same chronological split. Finally, Champernowne's decimal 0.123456789101112... is used as a constructed nonperiodic diagnostic control [5]. It is not part of the natural-constant transfer test.

### 2.6 Metrics

The primary metric is exact next-digit accuracy. Cross entropy and perplexity are also reported:

CE = -(1/N) Σ log p(x_t | context),   PPL = exp(CE).

Because consecutive sliding windows overlap heavily, token windows are not treated as independent statistical replicates. Seed-level variability is used descriptively for the neural model.

## 3. Results and Analysis

### 3.1 Pooled model: seen and unseen constants

The pooled TinyTCN achieved mean accuracy of **9.93%** on future digits of the four training constants and **9.99%** on the six completely unseen irrational constants. The IID control was **10.10%**. Descriptive 95% t intervals over the three training seeds were 9.56-10.30% for the seen group and 9.57-10.41% for the unseen group. The paired unseen-minus-IID difference was -0.11 percentage points with a descriptive 95% interval from -0.66 to 0.43 percentage points.

No tested unseen constant showed a stable improvement above the 10% reference across seeds. The best mean unseen result, ln 2 at 10.23%, is small and falls within the scale of seed-to-seed variation.

### 3.2 Cross entropy and n-gram baseline

Mean TinyTCN cross entropy was 2.30616 nats/digit on seen constants and 2.30636 on unseen constants, both **higher** than ln(10)=2.302585. In contrast, validation chose an order-0 n-gram model, whose average cross entropy was approximately 2.30255 on seen constants and 2.30263 on unseen constants. Thus adding local neural context did not improve probabilistic prediction; it slightly worsened it.

This is an important negative result. Accuracy alone could fluctuate around 10% by chance, whereas the cross-entropy result indicates that the TinyTCN also failed to assign systematically better probabilities to the true digits.

### 3.3 Single-constant versus pooled training

Across the four source constants, separate TinyTCNs achieved an average accuracy of **10.01%**, whereas the jointly trained model averaged **9.93%**. Pooling reduced average cross entropy from 2.31116 to 2.30616 nats/digit, suggesting a modest regularization effect, but this remained worse than the simple order-0 baseline and did not translate into better top-1 prediction.

Therefore, the data do not support the hypothesis that four-way joint training reveals a useful shared next-digit rule at this scale.

### 3.4 Structured nonperiodic diagnostic control

The Champernowne control produced a very different behavior. The TinyTCN reached **54.7%** exact next-digit accuracy, compared with **17.5%** for a majority-digit baseline and **29.7%** for a 4-gram model. This confirms that the architecture can exploit strong nonperiodic decimal structure.

However, its cross entropy was 3.624 nats/digit, worse than the 4-gram cross entropy of approximately 2.088. The neural model is therefore highly successful in top-1 prediction for many positions but overconfident when it is wrong. This illustrates why both accuracy and probabilistic metrics are necessary.

## 4. Discussion

The main finding is not that irrational constants are "random." The experiment establishes a narrower result: under a 32-digit raw-decimal representation, a 3,442-parameter causal TCN, approximately 24,000 training digits per source constant, and the tested future windows, no transferable next-digit structure was detected across the selected natural constants.

The failure of zero-shot transfer is informative because it tests a question not answered by a single-constant π experiment. If π, e, √2, and √3 shared a strong local digit mechanism accessible to a compact model, pooling their data might improve prediction or transfer to mathematically related unseen constants such as √5 or ln 2. Neither effect was observed.

The n-gram result reinforces this interpretation: validation selected order 0, meaning that orders 1-3 did not improve predictive cross entropy on the training-constant validation windows. At this scale, the useful information available to the tested models is effectively no better than marginal digit frequencies.

The Champernowne experiment prevents an overly broad interpretation that the TinyTCN is incapable of learning numerical sequences. It can exploit a structured nonperiodic decimal, but the same capability does not transfer to the selected natural constants. Champernowne is also known to be normal in base 10 [5], emphasizing that asymptotic frequency properties, local next-digit predictability, and algorithmic construction are distinct concepts.

These empirical findings should also be separated from mathematical normality. Normality of familiar constants such as π is not established, and statistical or neural-network experiments cannot prove it [4].

## 5. Limitations

1. The primary neural model is deliberately small; larger TCNs, Transformers, recurrent models, or state-space models could behave differently.
2. Only a 32-digit context is used in the confirmatory pilot.
3. Only decimal representation is tested.
4. Three neural training seeds provide limited uncertainty estimation.
5. The chosen constants are a designed benchmark, not a random sample from a formal population of irrational numbers.
6. The Champernowne control is structurally different from natural mathematical constants and is used only as a diagnostic.
7. The targeted novelty search is not a systematic review of every bibliographic database.
8. No result here proves randomness, normality, or theoretical unpredictability.

## 6. Conclusion

A lightweight TCN trained jointly on π, e, √2, and √3 did not achieve above-chance future next-digit prediction on the training constants or on six completely unseen irrational constants. Joint training did not improve accuracy relative to separate training, and a validation-selected order-0 n-gram baseline achieved better probabilistic calibration than the neural model. The same TinyTCN nevertheless extracted substantial top-1 structure from Champernowne's nonperiodic decimal.

The evidence therefore supports a limited but clear conclusion: **no transferable raw-decimal next-digit structure was detected across the tested natural irrational constants at this low-compute scale**. Future work should test longer contexts, alternative numerical bases, larger but still controlled models, and representations that expose more mathematical state.
